# College Composition and Postsecondary Writing

## Outcome

Produce a situated composing record that connects rhetorical purpose, audience, genre, evidence, process, revision, accessibility, conventions, and authorship.

## Workflow

1. Contract the rhetorical situation. Record audience, purpose, genre, context, stakes, discipline/community, medium, access conditions, local outcomes, and source/AI rules.
2. Read and inquire. Analyze the rhetorical situation and source set; identify claims, perspectives, evidence, gaps, and what the writer must learn before drafting.
3. Plan the composition. Choose a bounded purpose, claim or controlling idea, evidence strategy, organization, genre conventions, and medium. Mark project inference separately from source claims.
4. Compose. Draft for the named audience and genre; use sources with attribution; preserve uncertainty and avoid invented citations or evidence.
5. Revise through evidence. Use feedback, audience response, reverse outline, source audit, accessibility check, and revision history. Distinguish substantive revision from copyediting.
6. Reflect and disclose. Record decisions, feedback acted on, source and AI use, unresolved limitations, and what the writer—not the tool—decided.
7. Produce the learner artifact. Return rhetorical analysis, evidence map, draft, revision log, portfolio selection, reflective memo, or local-outcome crosswalk.
8. Run QA. Check audience/genre fit, source integrity, process evidence, accessibility, conventions appropriate to context, authorship, rights, and local calibration.

## Guardrails

- WPA 4.0 and AAC&U are frameworks and outcome bridges, not one universal score scale.
- Do not replace student authorship with ghostwriting or conceal material AI assistance.
- Do not infer competence from polished prose alone; inspect process and decisions.
- Respect multilingual, disability, dialect, and assistive-technology access while keeping the task construct explicit.
- Escalate grading, placement, academic-integrity, privacy, and institutional-policy questions to the accountable instructor or institution.

## Output contract

Return `rhetorical_contract`, `source_and_claim_map`, `genre_plan`, `draft_or_revision`, `feedback_record`, `revision_history`, `accessibility_notes`, `authorship_and_ai_log`, `reflection`, and `next_action` in the shared artifact envelope.

## Handoffs

- Route close interpretation to `close-reading-for-evidence`.
- Route evidence and claims to `information-primary-source-literacy` and `argumentation-reasoning-evidence`.
- Route inquiry/method questions to `quest-applied-research` or `advanced-research-independent-inquiry`.
